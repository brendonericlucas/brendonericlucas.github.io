#Jensen and Mark Gang Up on Quantum Computing

Recently, Jensen Huang of Nvidia and Mark Zuckerberg of Meta [managed to crater pretty much every quantum computing stock](https://www.fool.com/investing/2025/01/15/nvidia-jensen-huang-meta-mark-zuckerberg-stock-buy/) 
by means of a few off-the-cuff remarks each made pegging the advent of viable quantum computers at around 20 - 30 years from now. 

Now, [Nvidia has announced its first ever Quantum Day](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/nvidia-announces-first-ever-quantum-213014221.html), 
to take place in March of 2025. Nvidia also continues to ramp up the hiring of folks with the expertise necessary to contribute to efforts to produce large scale, error corrected quantum computers. 

What gives? 

I think we can safely ignore Zuck's opining on Joe Rogan. He is - by his own admission - "not much of an expert on Quantum Computing". 

But Jensen is a serious person. So why is he seemingly bearish on Quantum Computing while he at the same time continues to steer Nvidia towards it? 

I suspect that when Huang says that we're at least a couple of decades away from practically useful Quantum Computers, he mostly has in mind fully error corrected gated quantum computers. 

This would exclude approaches being pursued by the folks at [D-Wave](https://www.dwavesys.com/), whose quantum annealers don't actually use gates. 
The plus side of D-Wave's approach is that it's allowed them to produce substantially larger systems (more qubits) than we've thus far been able 
to produce using gated systems. The downside is that the only sorts of problems that quantum annealers seem to be able to tackle well are certain sorts 
of optimization problems. 

Figuring out how to build fully error corrected gated Quantum Computers is a _hard_ problem. Hard enough that Huang's skepticism is probably justified, 
even if it's true - as some have suggested - that Quantum Computing competes with AI shops for funding. But he's also smart enough to know that 
revolutionary technologies take time and money to develop, and that the spoils are likely to go to those who invest the requisite time and money. Hence
the ongoing ramp up of Nvidia's presence in the Quantum Computing space. 

In short, if you're long on Quantum Computing stocks, you're probably in reasonably good shape. But if you're expecting an imminent windfall, you might want to think again...
